## S3: Story architecture and retention map

### S3-0. How to read this stage

- **Chapter IDs:** the film has exactly six chapters, **C1–C6**. Claim IDs always have two digits (C01–C73), so "C1" is a chapter and "C01" is a claim. To avoid a third meaning, the S1 title-contract promise "C1" is renamed **TC1** here.
- **Mapping from the S0-C skeleton:** CH0 (cold open) and CH1 merge into **C1**. CH2 becomes C2, CH3 becomes C3, CH4 becomes C4, CH5 becomes C5 and CH6 becomes C6. Every S2 "Used in" entry follows this mapping, so CH0 becomes C1.
- **Scene clock:** 270 scenes, SC0001–SC0270, with whole-second nominal starts. The timestamps in sections C, D and E are locked anchors. S5 may move a scene boundary by up to ±2 s, but it may not move an anchored beat, and each segment must still end on its stated time.
- **Narration pace:** 150 wpm (2.5 words/s). The narrated words come to 6,210, and the 22 wordless scenes total 216 s, which matches S0-B.
- **AI reconstruction:** 0 s in every chapter (production rule 2). Every visual event is R1 (a graphic the editor builds) or R3 (Pexels, Pixabay, Videvo Free license, Coverr downloaded by hand, Mixkit Free License).

### A. Arc and logline

**Arc:** Success story (origin → the bet → near-failure → breakthrough → cost → transferable lesson). It opens cold on a defining contradiction, the way the biography arc does. A money ledger is the film's spine, borrowed from the money-and-scale arc's withheld-answer ladder: six sourced entries, and the full ledger is withheld until 37:50.

**Logline:** His father died when he was seven. His family's share of the barbershop was five rupees. From there, a Bengaluru barber turned one used van into a luxury rental fleet and, by most accounts, bought a Rolls-Royce Ghost in 2011. By his own account, the road tax that followed almost bankrupted him. As of the latest reports, he still cuts hair. This film follows the money, one sourced entry at a time.

**Central question (said aloud in B4):** How does a man get from one used van to a Rolls-Royce without ever putting down the scissors?

### F. Emotion curve

- **C1 The Shop:** a jolt of curiosity (the contradiction), then a grief that is held back, never milked, then a quiet, practical resolve. The low point is SC0021 (wordless, empty shop).
- **C2 The Chair:** reclaiming. Steady pride in craft and discipline, warm and unhurried.
- **C3 One Used Van:** worry (a loan he can't keep up with), then relief (the rental idea), then momentum (the first client, seven cars).
- **C4 The Luxury Bet:** tension at the turn, then confidence as the company becomes official and the first luxury car arrives. The curve rises.
- **C5 The Ghost and the Bill:** awe, then creeping dread, then the low point of the film (36:30, "almost bankrupt"), then relief (he kept the cars), then a wry, delighted beat (500–750 haircuts).
- **C6 Still at the Chair:** clarity (what the record does and doesn't support), then earned respect, then warmth at the payoff. The close is reflective and lands on the present.
