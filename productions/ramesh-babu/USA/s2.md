## S2: Claim ledger

**How to read the ledger**
- **Language:** the client language is American English, so the engine's two wording columns (client language and English) are one column here.
- **Batch size:** production rule 1 asks for the whole film in one table, which overrides the engine's 25-claim batch limit.
- **Order:** claims follow their planned first use, chapter by chapter (CH0–CH6 from the S0-C skeleton). S3 and S5 bind each claim to SC IDs.
- **Confidence:** every source is a search-engine excerpt, and no article was read in full. Confidence is therefore capped at **medium**, with one exception: the company registry record (C33), which three registry sites agree on. LEAD-02 (read every article in full) can raise a claim's confidence. It can never lower a wording rule below what this table allows.
- **Money (version lock):**
  - Every rupee figure appears on screen in **US dollars** as the headline number. The rupee original is shown small, with a chip giving the year-matched rate.
  - The research gives no exchange rates and no CPI values. Every dollar value is therefore **PENDING (LEAD-03)**. Each ESTIMATE row below states its rupee amount, year, rate series and formula, so the number can be filled in without new judgment. No number is guessed.
  - Rupee amounts in digits: 1 lakh = ₹100,000; 1 crore = ₹10,000,000.
- **Quotes:** none is verified word for word. Every QUOTE row is a **paraphrase with attribution** ("he told YourStory in 2014 that…") and never appears as a verbatim quote on screen.
- **Wording rule:** "Wording rule" is what narration and on-screen text may say. Anything the rule does not allow is out of the film.
