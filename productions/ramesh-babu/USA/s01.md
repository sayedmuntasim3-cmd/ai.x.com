**How to find less common stock (production rule 5)**
1. Search with 2–4 plain nouns plus a light word, for example "straight razor leather strop close", "old barber chair chrome footrest", "steering wheel hands night rain", "car keys wooden counter", "ledger handwriting fountain pen top view". Place names are used only for place-literal searches. Go past the first page of results. On Pixabay, sort by newest rather than popular. On Pexels, filter to 4K and browse contributor profiles (for example Anil Sharma's Bengaluru clips). On Videvo, filter to the Free license. Browse Mixkit and Coverr category pages by hand, past the first screen.
2. Run one frame from each candidate through a reverse image search (Google Lens or TinEye). Drop the clip if that frame appears across many YouTube thumbnails or ads.
3. Prefer clips with practical light, slight handheld movement, shallow depth of field, people who are incidental or unidentifiable, and 4K or higher resolution. Reject clips with looks to camera, posed smiles, the over-saturated "commercial" grade, burned-in text or watermarks.
4. Mute every stock clip and rebuild the ambience from Pixabay and Mixkit SFX.
5. Log each clip: URL, contributor, license page saved as PDF, download date, resolution, fps.

### 5.2 Angle statement

- **Angle:** Most retellings stop at "barber buys a Rolls-Royce." This film follows the money, putting every loan, client and tax bill on one sourced ledger. It also shows where the record is solid and where the headlines outgrew it.
- **Central question:** How does a man get from one used van to a Rolls-Royce without ever putting down the scissors?
- **Why now:** The reported fleet is still growing; Business Today and Daily Jagran reported a Range Rover added in October 2025. So are the numbers attached to him online, from 67 cars to 600, plus a "billionaire" label that no filing supports. The film sorts out the record.
- **Who it is for:** US viewers who follow self-made business stories, car culture and money breakdowns.
- **What the viewer feels:**
  - Minute 1: curiosity fixed on a contradiction: a Rolls-Royce and a barber's chair, one man.
  - Midpoint: tension, as ordinary rentals fill with competitors and he bets on luxury in 2004.
  - End: earned respect and one practical takeaway: he kept the trade that started everything.
