# Watch checklist: turning the scorecard's [O] scores into evidence (30 minutes)

Companion to `docs/research/REFERENCE_SCORECARD.md`. That doc scores the ten films from metadata and
marks every judgement it could not verify as `[O]` (opinion). This file is the 30-minute watch that
closes the gap: **10 films × first 3 minutes**, one row per fact, one scoring rule per dimension.

Nothing here needs a subscription, an API or an account. A phone, a stopwatch and the YouTube page are
enough. Total: 30 minutes of watching + about 10 minutes of writing.

## How to run it

1. Open the film on YouTube, set quality to 480p (enough to read a source line), turn the volume to a
   comfortable level and **do not skip the pre-roll** — the film starts after it.
2. Start the stopwatch when the first frame of the film appears, not when the ad ends.
3. Watch 0:00-3:00 once, without pausing, with the table below open. Write tick marks, not sentences.
4. After 3:00, pause, count the ticks, and fill the six score cells with the rules in §2.
5. Optional and outside the 30 minutes: for the "ending" dimension, jump to the last 60 seconds and
   note what it does (the scorecard's ending scores are all `[O]` too).

Do the films in the order of the scorecard so the numbering matches. If time runs short, skip #8
(RMTransit) first — it is the lowest-ratio film of the ten — and write that it was skipped.

## 1. What to tick (one page per film)

Legend: `A` = archive / historical footage or still · `F` = the film-maker's own footage ·
`S` = generic stock · `G` = graphic, map, diagram or animation · `T` = talking head or on-camera host

```text
Film #___  ______________________________  link ______________________________

0:00-0:30  HOOK
  first visual after 0:00 (what is on screen): ______________________________________
  is the title's promise spoken or shown before 0:30?   yes / no   at 0:____
  a number, a date or a count of lives in the first 30 s?  yes / no  which: ____________
  cuts in 0:00-0:30 (count each change of shot): ______

0:00-3:00  BODY
  total cuts in 3:00: ______        cuts per minute = ______ / 3 = ______
  longest single shot: ______ s (what is it: ____________________________________)
  shot mix ticks, first 3 min:  A ___  F ___  S ___  G ___  T ___
  first map / diagram / cutaway at: ______  (none = write "none")
  on-screen source lines (a credit, archive name, document title, date): ______
  first source line at: ______
  music: starts at ______ (none = "none") · audible under speech?  yes / no
  narration: first person / third person · host on camera? yes / no
  filler or hedging heard ("basically", "kind of", "I think"): ______

LAST 60 s (optional, outside the 30 min)
  what the film does at the end: restates the answer / new topic / sponsorship / fades out
  sources named in the last minute: ______
```

## 2. The scoring rules (so the second viewer gets the same number)

Apply these to the ticks. If a cell cannot be filled, write `—` and say why — never guess.

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook, first 30 s** | nothing happens, logo or title card | generic b-roll, promise not stated | promise shown or stated, no stake | promise + a concrete stake (number, date, count) before 0:30 | promise + stake + the film's question, all inside 0:30 |
| **Central question** | none; it is a topic, not a question | implied only in the title | asked once, on screen or in narration | asked and restated as the film's spine | asked, restated, and answered by name at the end |
| **Pacing, cuts/min** | < 4 | 4-7 | 8-12 | 13-20 | > 20, or no shot longer than 4 s |
| **Visuals** | one still or one shot for the whole 3 min | mostly stock, few changes | mixed stock and graphics, uneven | archive or own footage carries it, graphics used with purpose | archive/own footage + graphics + on-screen evidence, no filler shot |
| **Maps / diagrams** | none in 3 min | one, decorative, unlabelled | one, labelled, before 3:00 | two or more, each doing work | a diagram is the argument, and it is on screen before 1:30 |
| **Sources shown** | none, and none in the description | one, vague ("experts say") | one named source on screen | two or more named on screen | every archive/clip carries a source line, plus a list in the description |
| **Narration** | unscripted rambling | scripted but flat, filler heard | clear, plain, some filler | written and delivered with intent, no filler | written, delivered and paced to the pictures; host is the writer |
| **Music / sound** | none, or a loop that fights the voice | music, but no ducking | bed present, mostly under control | composed or chosen score that tracks the beats | score + designed silence/effects; music never covers a word |
| **Ending** | stops | fades without a point | restates the topic | answers the question and names the next film | answers the question, names sources, leaves one line to remember |

## 3. What to do with the numbers

- Replace the `[O]` cells in `REFERENCE_SCORECARD.md` §2 with these scores and change the tag to `[M]`,
  citing the tick that produced it (e.g. "cuts/min 14 → 4, measured 0:00-3:00").
- Where a measured score contradicts the old `[O]` score, keep both in the row for one revision:
  `3 [O] → 2 [M]`. That shows the checklist did work.
- Then re-read §3 and §5 of the scorecard: **any rule in §5 that the measured data no longer supports
  must be changed**, not defended. The rules most at risk are 1 (cut every 6 s), 2 (diagram before
  1:30) and 8 (no silence over 0.8 s) — they were craft floors, not measurements.
- Update §6 to say which films were watched and by whom, with the date.

## 5. The one thing this checklist cannot fix

The scorecard's §0 limit stands: no automated session could load playback from the build machine
(login wall). This checklist is written for a **person with a browser**, which is why it is ticks and
stopwatch rather than a script. If a future session does have the YouTube Data API available, only
`statistics` and `contentDetails` come back from it — not cuts, not on-screen sources — so the watch
above is still the only way to fill those rows.
