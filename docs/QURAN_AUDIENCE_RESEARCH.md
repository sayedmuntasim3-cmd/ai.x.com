What was looked up, what it shows, and what is not known. Nothing here is a promise of views. Figures
from third-party trackers (vidIQ, OutlierKit) are their estimates, not YouTube's own numbers; retention
cannot be read from outside a channel (vidIQ says so itself).

## What this means for the system (decisions, to be tested with the channel's own analytics)

1. **Original work is required, not only good for views.** A film built on another reciter's widely posted
   recording risks being "reused content". The owner's own recitation (or a reciter who gives written
   permission) plus original direction (meaning-led shots, verified Arabic, the review) is the safe base.
   The CC0 / public-domain recordings used so far are for testing only.
2. **The recitation carries the video.** The largest numbers found belong to famous reciters and to a
   plain-wall format: the voice and the recitation matter more than footage. The mastering engine and the
   rule "the recitation is the only sound" stay central; pictures support, never compete.
3. **Long form and Shorts are different products.** Long form: whole surahs or long passages, calm pacing,
   chapters per ayah (already generated). Shorts and Reels: one passage of 20-45 s (the measured median
   Short was 32 s), a strong first picture from the first second, the Arabic visible, no intro. The renderer
   already makes 9:16; cutting a passage on sure ayah ends is the next step.
4. **The first seconds:** the surah name over a real picture from frame one (done), the first ayah begins at
   once; no spoken intro, no music, no clickbait title.
5. **Measure, don't assume:** after publishing, read retention and traffic sources in YouTube Studio for each
   format and compare; this file records only what was found from outside.
