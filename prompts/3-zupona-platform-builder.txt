0. MISSION
==================================================================
Build ai.zupona.com into the world's strongest autonomous documentary studio.
- A client types a topic and picks any duration from 1 minute to 5+ hours.
- 100 specialist AI agents in 10 squads research, write, source, clear rights, generate, voice, edit, grade, mix, check, render and package the film.
- No human approval step in production. Every decision a human used to make becomes a deterministic, fail-closed machine gate.
- Speed: a 40-minute film is ready (QA-passed 4K master, packaging, Shorts) in 20-30 minutes of wall time, p50. Publishing follows automatically after the platform's own checks (outside that window).
- Quality: every film is engineered to give a brand-new channel's first upload a real chance at 1M+ views. An engineering target, never a marketing promise (section 11).
- Positioning: the only autonomous maker of researched, cited, rights-tracked long documentaries in any language, Bangla first.

3. ARCHITECTURE (real stack)
==================================================================
3.1 Control plane: Next.js 16 on vinext, deployed as the single Cloudflare Worker. UI and API at ai.zupona.com (check APPS.md and wrangler.jsonc routes; a new host must be added to routes). Guard every studio route and server action server-side with the existing session helpers; proxy routing is not authorisation.
3.2 Orchestration: one Durable Object per film job holds the state machine (stages, chapter fan-out, budgets, retries, deadlines). Cloudflare Queues carry work items. Provider jobs are asynchronous: poll by stored job id and resume after a crash without resubmitting a paid request (idempotency keys on every paid call).
3.3 Data: D1 holds projects, chapters, scenes, shots, claims, assets, the rights ledger, provider routes and terms, prices, gate results and run metrics. New tables come through db/migrations/*.sql. CI does NOT apply migrations: run npx wrangler d1 execute zupona-v3-db --remote --file=... and --local, or use the Apply D1 migration workflow, before shipping code that depends on them.
3.4 Storage: R2 for originals, generated assets, reference packs, signed licences, chunks, masters and stems, keyed by content hash for reuse across chapters, films and clients where the licence allows. Media in caches.default as the shop does. Bindings only through src/lib/db.ts.
3.5 AI routing: Cloudflare AI Gateway as the single adapter for logs, spend caps and fallbacks; direct keys where gateways lag (Kling Singapore Elements, Gemini Omni multi-turn editing, ElevenLabs v4 voices).
3.6 Render pool: FFmpeg on GPUs (NVENC) renders 10-minute chunks in parallel, cut at chapter or scene boundaries, closed GOPs, identical encode settings, concatenated without re-encode. Mediabunny for in-browser preview, probing, demux/mux and light client-side assembly. Final 4K masters from FFmpeg. Carry C2PA and SynthID provenance through the pipeline (re-encodes strip it) and sign every master with a Zupona C2PA manifest declaring AI use.
3.7 Motion graphics and maps, MIT/Apache tools: Motion Canvas, lottie-web, MapLibre GL (globe projection), CesiumJS, rendered headless. Remotion's free licence covers individuals and for-profit companies with up to 3 employees; larger companies need a Company License, and the licence forbids selling a derivative of Remotion itself; do not build on it without a Company License. Do not automate Google Earth Studio until its terms allow it; Earth flyovers come from MapLibre/Cesium with licensed or open imagery and visible attribution ("© OpenStreetMap contributors" for ODbL data). Fonts: OFL or licensed for video.
3.8 Speed rules from AGENTS.md apply to the studio UI: StoreImage for pictures; paginated grids; prefetch={false} on long lists; Promise.all for independent D1 reads; measure with Playwright (FCP, transferred bytes, long tasks under CPU throttling).

4. THE AUTONOMOUS PIPELINE AND THE 20-30 MINUTE BUDGET (40-minute film)
==================================================================
Wall-clock targets from submit; stages overlap.
T+0:00-2:00 Viral Story Builders: beat-the-best teardown; topic score; angle; named-subject and sensitive-subject risk scan; title contract; 3 titles; 3 thumbnail concepts; claim ledger via allow-listed sources; arc and chapter plan (4 chapters of about 10 min); bibles and tokens.
T+1:30-6:00: chapter scripts in parallel (one Story Builders cell per chapter, shared bible and loop ledger); script QA (facts, named-subject rules, banned list, native-language check) per chapter as each lands.
T+3:00-8:00 Audio Engineers: TTS per chapter in parallel with word timestamps; lexicon applied; loudness pre-normalisation. Narration timing is the clock every visual is cut to.
T+2:30-14:00 Media Hunters + Rights Guards: about 216 shot slots (ceil(40 x 4.5 x 1.2)); search fanned across providers; licence classes resolved automatically; agency and creator licence requests issued asynchronously, each with a pre-chosen fallback, so the timeline never waits; a licence arriving later can swap a shot in a post-release re-render.
T+3:00-14:00 AI Creators: keyframes first from reference packs, then image-to-video or reference-to-video. R9 budget: at most 20% of runtime (35% only for pre-photographic chapters). For a 40-minute film that is at most 480 s of disclosed reconstruction on screen; with 1.6x over-generation, at most about 770 billed AI seconds, split between AI motion (3-6 s shots) and AI stills animated with parallax. That is about 80-160 clips. ComfyUI's Kling node estimates about 164 s per image-to-video job and 319 s per text-to-video job (estimator constants, not measured), so run 25-50 concurrent jobs across at least 3 providers, re-routed by queue depth and failure rate, and measure real latency per provider.
T+8:00-18:00 Visual Enhancers (upscale AI segments only, grade, parallax and L5 variants, FX templates) and Timeline Composers (per-chapter EDL against narration word timestamps).
T+12:00-24:00 Render Optimizers: 4 chunks in parallel on the GPU pool; Quality Checkers run per-chunk gates while others render.
T+22:00-28:00: concat, whole-film gates, packaging finalised against the real cut, Shorts and Facebook cut, stems, SRT, FCPXML/EDL export.
Then publishing (section 9).
Scaling rule: duration scales with parallelism, not wall time. A 5-hour film is about 30 chapter blocks across the pool; report its expected time and cost before the client spends.

6. MEDIA AND RIGHTS (fail-closed, no human gate)
==================================================================
6.1 Discovery versus source. Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X and YouTube go on a denylist in the asset fetcher: never a file source, no headless or logged-in sessions on these domains, and the fetcher blocks them for viewing as well as downloading. Leads come only from public web-search results, official APIs within their terms, UGC-agency and archive catalogues, or links the client pastes; a lead stores the post URL, creator handle and agency credit, never media. Never crop watermarks, never reuse social audio, never feed a scraped frame into image-to-video.
6.2 The ladder: R1 owned/procedural; R2 public domain, government or CC0 with item-level checks (South Asian government material is not automatically PD); R3 Pexels/Pixabay/Mixkit/Videvo/CC-BY (Coverr and Unsplash stay off inside the automated platform until a legal review clears them: Coverr's terms bar feeding a video-creation service and Unsplash's API guidelines restrict this use, per the PR #57 audit) (Pexels key issuance paused per the repo matrix; Pixabay requires a 24-hour cache); R4 subscription stock only under platform/reseller terms covering end clients' channels; R5 premium/editorial per asset; R6 news archives (AP, Reuters, British Pathé); R7 UGC agencies (Newsflare, ViralHog, Storyful, Collab, Caters, Jukin/Trusted Media Brands) with Content ID allowlisting of the client channel; R8 direct creator e-sign licence; R9 disclosed AI; R10 fair use, OFF.
R8 flow: outreach by email, comment or human-sent DM (Meta blocks automated cold DMs); outreach emails identify Zupona, give a postal address and an opt-out, at most one message plus one reminder; comments and DMs only from a human-operated account; click-through licence; ownership check (account-control code plus camera-original upload); creator 18 or over (parent co-signs for footage with minors); payment; signed PDF + IP + timestamp + SHA-256 stored in R2, under a privacy notice and stated retention period.
Order by slot: evidence R2 -> R6 -> R7 -> R8 -> R5 -> R1 -> R9; illustrative R1 -> R3 -> R4 -> R9 -> R5.
Client tier gates paid rungs: free R1-R3 + R9; pro + R4; premium/enterprise + R5-R8; a hard per-film spend cap on every tier.
6.3 Licence classes that clear automatically: US-GOV-PD (with an item check), CC0, PDM; CC-BY / CC-BY-SA (attribution generated with title, author, source, licence link and "modified" where true; BY-SA never in L5 variants or thumbnails); OGL-3.0; Pexels or Pixabay licence; a stock-API receipt covering end clients; an agency licence; a creator e-sign. Auto-SKIPPED: unknown, NC, ND, editorial-in-promotional-context, anything social-sourced, any identifiable person in a sensitive-context slot without a release permitting sensitive use, and free-stock identifiable people, logos or artwork in thumbnails.
6.4 Rights ledger columns, in addition to existing ones: license_class, licensor, license_id or receipt_url, end_client_covered, editorial_only, modification_allowed, ai_use_allowed, model_release, attribution_text, territory, term, content_id_whitelist_status, source_file_sha256, signed_release_url, caption_source_url (named people), retrieved_at.
6.5 Unknowns to resolve with partners, building adapters behind feature flags until answered: per-clip prices for agencies and archives; reseller/sublicensing terms (Getty, Shutterstock, Adobe, Storyblocks, Pond5); AI-use clauses in stock licences; programmatic licensing APIs for Pathé, AP and Reuters. This is research, not legal advice: counsel must approve the creator licence and release templates for the US, UK and Bangladesh before launch.

8. QUALITY GATES (automatic; CRITICAL gates block release)
==================================================================
G1 Promise-payoff (CRITICAL): every title/thumbnail promise maps to a payoff scene; an LLM judge reads the final transcript.
G2 Hook: four beats within 30 s; title proven by 1:15.
G3 Loop ledger: every loop closed; at least one open until the final payoff.
G4 Cadence: reset spacing from the EDL; visual events per minute measured with FFmpeg scdet against calibrated per-section thresholds; midpoint and cliffhanger within ±5% of 50% and 80%.
G5 Facts and named subjects (CRITICAL): every on-screen number and claim has a sourced claim id; contested figures as ranges; allegations need a primary record or two independent sources, are attributed, include the response or a dated "no public response" line; no quote without a verbatim source; private individuals anonymised; no accusatory words about named living people or companies without a court or regulator finding.
G6 Rights (CRITICAL): every shot allowlisted; no social-sourced file; editorial restrictions respected; attribution rendered; music and final mix fingerprint-clean.
G7 AI share and disclosure (CRITICAL): within the per-chapter cap; disclosed in the platform setting, nothing written on the video; none in the first 30 s when real material exists; none in banned categories; synthetic flag set whenever the film has a realistic R9 shot, a synthetic or cloned voice of a real person, or altered real footage; C2PA manifest signed; EU AI Act Art. 50 (check the current application date) and India's synthetic-content labelling rules (check scope) tracked per target market; Meta AI label set for photorealistic AI video and realistic AI audio.
G8 Identity consistency: embedding similarity of each R9 shot against its reference pack, re-roll below a calibrated threshold. Face and voice embeddings are computed only for synthetic composite characters and consented voice clones, never to identify real people; the embedding model's licence must allow commercial use.
G9 Voice-visual sync: anchor visual lands 0.3 s before to 0.5 s after the word timestamp; spoken numbers on screen; names spelled identically across narration, subtitles, graphics and thumbnail.
G10 Repetition: no asset hash or region within 10 minutes; at most 3 L5 variants per source per film.
G11 Audio: two-pass loudnorm to -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP; ebur128 report; music ducked under VO with sidechaincompress; silencedetect flags dead air except scripted silences.
G12 Text: Bangla conjunct shaping through the libass/HarfBuzz path with a render test of real strings; subtitles match narration word for word.
G13 Translation fidelity: each extra audio track back-translated and compared to the master; no synthetic re-voicing of real people's speech.
G14 Originality (CRITICAL for monetisation): cross-film and cross-client similarity of script, structure, opening and shot sequence below threshold; original analysis in every film. YouTube's 'inauthentic content' policy (renamed 15 July 2025) removes monetisation channel-wide for mass-produced, templated output.
G15 Visual quality: a VLM 'AI-slop' check on R9 shots (anatomy, text artefacts, plastic skin, drift); literalness check on evidence slots; no identifiable stock person over negative narration.
G16 Policy and sensitive subjects: advertiser-friendliness pre-check; minors, sexual violence, suicide (helpline card), extremism, graphic content and religion-neutrality checks; finance and health disclaimers; no encouragement of illegal access; clickbait over-promise check; paid-promotion disclosure on sponsored films.
G17 Technical (ffprobe): resolution, fps, codec, A/V drift under 40 ms, no black or frozen frames, chapter markers valid.
G18 Beat-the-best: each testable commitment from the teardown is shown delivered, with scene ids.

Per the Data API revision history (4 Dec 2025), a video upload now costs about 100 units, not the former ~1,600. Set the altered/synthetic flag (recalled as status.containsSyntheticMedia; confirm the field in current docs) and 'made for kids' = No for general-audience films.
9.2 Flow: run an audio and video fingerprint pre-check before upload; upload private; poll checks. Check which claim signals the Data API exposes to a non-partner channel (status.rejectionReason, contentDetails.regionRestriction, processingDetails). If claims cannot be read, keep the video private for the platform's check window, publish only when every clip has a licence-ledger row, and show the client a one-screen Studio checklist for any claim the API cannot see.
On a Content ID claim: (1) if the claimant is the licensor or its agency, request allowlisting or a release under the stored licence; (2) dispute only when a stored record proves the segment is public domain, Zupona-generated or licensed for this channel, and cite that record; (3) if the claimant reinstates the claim, never appeal automatically (a rejected appeal lets the claimant issue a takedown and a copyright strike): swap the segment from the next rung, re-render and replace the upload, or trim with YouTube's editor; (4) never dispute or remove claims in bulk. The video stays private while any claim is open. Then publish, schedule or premiere.
9.3 Thumbnail Test & Compare and title tests are unconfirmed in the API: build the API path behind a flag with a fallback one-screen client checklist in YouTube Studio.
9.4 Multi-language audio tracks: API support unknown; same flag-plus-fallback approach.
9.5 Facebook: one original upload per Page (no duplicate cross-posting); a native 9:16 or 4:5 cut of 60-180 s with a sound-off 3-second visual hook and burned-in captions; 1-2 line caption, at most 3 hashtags; full-film link in the first comment; AI label for photorealistic AI; branded-content tool on sponsored films. Monetisation eligibility in Bangladesh is unknown: never promise Facebook revenue.
9.6 Publication risk class. G5, G14 and G16 assign every film a class. LOW: no allegations against named living people or existing companies and no crime, health, election or religion focus; publishes automatically after platform checks. HIGH: produced just as fast, but the engine first rewrites the angle under the named-subject rules. If a film is still HIGH, the master is uploaded private and the client makes it public with one tap after accepting a two-line statement that they are the publisher. This is not a production approval step; it exists because when Zupona both writes and publishes a film, Zupona is the publisher (laws that shield platforms for user content, such as 47 U.S.C. 230, do not cover content the service creates). Zupona's terms must include an acceptable-use policy, client warranties for every uploaded file, an indemnity, a designated DMCA agent registered with the US Copyright Office, and a repeat-infringer policy.

10. FEATURES THAT BEAT INVIDEO (re-verify every competitor fact live before marketing use)
==================================================================
- True long form, 1 minute to 5+ hours, as chapter blocks. InVideo's practical limits (monthly AI minutes, seconds-long generative clips) make feature length expensive there, and it is not marketed for it (unverified).
- A citation layer: claim ledger, on-screen source chips, sources in the description, a render block when a key claim has no source.
- A rights ledger per asset plus a pre-publish Content ID shield.
- Archive and public-domain integrations (NASA, LOC, Smithsonian, NARA, DVIDS, Europeana, Commons, South Asian collections with item checks), so visuals match the narration instead of generic stock.
- Anti-repetition gate and a VLM narration-match check.
- Consistency across a full film: bibles, reference packs, tokens, identity QA, repair.
- Bangla first: native narrator, pronunciation lexicon, conjunct shaping, multi-track English/Hindi/other audio from one master.
- Packaging intelligence: beat-the-best teardown, outlier topic scoring, title contract, 3 thumbnails for Test & Compare, Shorts and a Facebook cut, pinned comment, localisation.
- Honest pricing: flat published price per finished minute; cost estimate before spend (the review receipt already in AI_VIDEO_STUDIO_UX_ROADMAP.md); QA-failed renders free; single-scene regeneration at scene cost; no watermark on paid output.
- Text-command edits after delivery ('make chapter 2 darker', 'replace SC0014', 'shorter by 3 minutes'), each re-running only affected scenes and chunks.
- Pro export: FCPXML/EDL, stems, SRT, 9:16 and 4:5 cut-downs.
- Live speed: a job timeline with per-squad progress and predicted finish time.
- Kutly-style single-screen composer, already adopted (Idea and Voiceover paths, review receipt, distinct job states). Never copy Kutly's brand, wording or assets.
- Brand-documentary mode: sponsored films tick 'includes paid promotion', use Meta's branded-content tool, show 'Sponsored by [brand]' / 'স্পন্সর: [ব্র্যান্ড]' on screen in the first 30 s and in the description, are never framed as independent investigations, never make a sponsor's competitors or critics the subject of allegations, and back any comparative claim with documented, current evidence (false-advertising law, e.g. US Lanham Act s.43(a)).
- Every public comparison uses only facts measured in the published benchmark (P9), dated, with the method linked; rivals' names are used only to identify them.

12. PHASES AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
==================================================================
Each phase is its own PR (stacked on #50/#57/#58 as appropriate), with tests, docs, System Guide updates and migrations applied local and remote before dependent code ships. Commit subjects readable by the owner.
P0 Reconcile. Work: resolve C1-C4; update docs; remap the registry to 10 product squads (100 roles); save the 10 pasted squad prompts verbatim to src/lib/documentary-squad-prompts/<squad_slug>.v1.md and load them into D1 as versioned rows. Accept when: the registry exports 10 squads x 10 roles; the old-to-new mapping table is committed; the 10 prompt files exist and match the pasted text byte for byte; DOCUMENTARY_ENGINE_V4.md and the orchestration docs describe R9 and the machine rights policy; the System Guide changelog has a row.
P1 Story and packaging engine. Work: the Prompt A protocol (S0-S7) as a server pipeline with JSON outputs; beat-the-best teardown; claim ledger with web research; named-subject and sensitive-subject rules; title contract. Accept when, on 10 test topics across 5 genres: 100% of claims typed and sourced or marked verify; G1, G2, G3 and G5 pass automatically; a planted accusatory title about a named company is rewritten automatically; a 40-minute script plus manifest completes in 6 minutes or less.
P2 Media ladder and rights. Work: adapters R1-R6 live; R7/R8 behind flags; fail-closed licence classifier; social-domain denylist (viewing and downloading); rights ledger; model licence gate (7.0). Accept when: 216 slots for a 40-minute film fill in 12 minutes or less with zero non-allowlisted classes; evidence slots 100% literal or a disclosed reconstruction; unit tests cover every licence class, denylist case and sensitive-context skip.
P3 Voice, music and sync. Work: TTS with word timestamps, lexicon, music, SFX, ducking, loudness, fingerprint check. Accept when: the Bangla narrator reaches the chosen MOS bar in a blind panel of at least 10 native listeners; G9 and G11 pass on 5 films; a planted sound-alike music prompt is rejected.
P4 AI Creators and consistency. Work: bibles, reference packs, keyframe-first generation, router, identity QA, repair. Accept when: 95% or more of R9 shots pass identity similarity within 2 rolls; R9 share within the per-chapter cap; every R9 shot disclosed through the platform setting; zero R9 shots in banned categories.
P5 Timeline, enhancement and render pool. Work: EDL assembly, FX templates (FX-MAP, FX-ARCHIVE, FX-PAPER, FX-KINETIC, FX-FILM, FX-SPLIT, FX-COUNTER, FX-SOURCECHIP, FX-RECON), grade, chunked GPU render, concat, C2PA signing. Accept when: a 40-minute 4K master renders in 12 minutes or less on the pool; G17 passes; seams invisible and inaudible in frame-diff and audio-diff checks; the C2PA manifest validates.
P6 QA gates. Work: G1-G18 with the targeted fix loop and the publication risk class. Accept when: each of 20 planted defects in a test film is caught; fewer than 5% of fixes regress.
P7 Publishing. Work: audit filed; private-first upload; claim handling per 9.2; synthetic flag; Shorts and Facebook paths; publication risk class. Accept when: end-to-end publish runs on a test channel; a planted claim triggers an automatic swap and re-render with no appeal sent; a planted HIGH-risk film is rewritten automatically or held private until the client publishes it.
P8 Speed and scale. Work: concurrency tuning, multi-provider re-routing, caching. Accept when: 40-minute p50 is 30 minutes or less over 20 consecutive runs, with p90 recorded; a 3-hour film completes with its predicted time shown up front; cost per minute matches the estimate within 15%.
P9 Feedback loop and the InVideo benchmark. Work: analytics ingestion; packaging and topic updates; a same-topic head-to-head against InVideo (and Pictory/Fliki) on 5 topics. Accept when: the benchmark report (time, cost, citation coverage, repetition, Bangla MOS, blind viewer preference) is published internally with its method; every marketing claim links to it.
